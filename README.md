SmartExpense is a web-based expense tracking application built to help small businesses and individual professionals manage, categorize, and export their financial transactions more efficiently. This repository captures how the Beta Launch was managed from a quality assurance and project leadership perspective using Agile principles and GitHub project tools.

The goal of this project was not just to find bugs, but to think strategically about how software quality impacts business performance. As the project lead, I used GitHub Issues and a Kanban board to organize work, prioritize tasks based on business risk, and ensure accountability throughout the testing lifecycle. In addition to managing issues, I leveraged AI to draft a structured system test plan and evaluate automated testing tools that would make sense for a non-technical business team.

The repository includes five core issues: two high-priority bugs and three feature enhancements. Each issue was labeled by type and priority to reflect its impact on the business. For example, a session timeout vulnerability was marked high priority because of its security implications, while incorrect monthly calculations were flagged due to the risk of financial inaccuracies. On the feature side, role-based permissions and CSV export functionality were prioritized because they directly support business usability and accounting workflows.

To manage progress, I created a Kanban board with four columns: To Do, In Progress, QA/Testing, and Done. This setup keeps everything visible and reinforces a simple but effective workflow—nothing is complete until it has passed testing. The board also reflects how Agile project management supports clarity, transparency, and team alignment.

From a testing perspective, I designed a hybrid approach. Manual testing plays an important role in validating real user behavior, checking calculations, and exploring edge cases that automated scripts might miss. However, relying solely on manual checks isn’t scalable. That’s why automation was introduced as part of the QA strategy.

When evaluating automation tools, I compared developer-focused tools like Selenium with more business-friendly platforms such as Katalon. While Selenium is powerful and highly customizable, it requires programming knowledge and ongoing maintenance. For a cross-functional or non-technical team, Katalon makes more sense. Its low-code interface, built-in reporting, and easier onboarding process reduce reliance on developers and make automation more accessible from a management standpoint. Strategically, this lowers long-term costs and improves efficiency.

This project also embraces the concept of Shift-Left Testing, which means introducing testing earlier in the development process instead of waiting until the end. The earlier issues are identified, the cheaper and easier they are to fix. From a business perspective, this reduces rework, protects timelines, and lowers the risk of post-launch failures that could damage reputation or revenue.

Overall, this repository demonstrates how Agile workflows, structured QA planning, and AI-assisted strategy can support better business outcomes. Rather than treating testing as a technical afterthought, this project approaches it as a leadership responsibility—balancing risk, cost, scalability, and quality to support a successful product launch.
